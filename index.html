Ultimate Pinochle Privacy Policy

Effective October 19, 2026

Who we are

Ultimate Pinochle is a card game published by Metropolis III. This policy explains what information the game handles, why, who else processes it, and how to delete it. If you have a question, write to jkirby1325@gmail.com.

The short version

  • The game does not ask for your email address, real name, phone number or location.
  • There are no ads, no analytics or tracking, and your information is never sold.
  • Playing alone against the computer needs no sign-in. Once you are signed in, your single-player statistics are saved with your profile.
  • Playing online needs a free account (a username and password) with Unity Gaming Services, which stores your profile so it follows you between devices.
  • Photos you upload are checked automatically for adult or violent content before other players can see them, and chat is filtered for offensive words. Breaking the rules can lead to warnings and bans from online play.
  • You can delete your account and everything stored with it from inside the game: Online, then Account, then Delete account.

Signing in

The game signs you in as a guest with Unity Authentication, which gives you a random player ID. A guest can play alone; to play online you make an account with a username and password, so your profile follows you to other devices. Your password goes to Unity and is never stored by the game; a sign-in token is kept on your device so you stay signed in.

You can also link a Unity account (Unity Player Accounts), which you sign in to on Unity's own web page with your email address, as a way back in if you forget your password. Your email address stays with Unity: the game receives only a sign-in token, never the address.

Your profile

Stored with your player ID in Unity Cloud Save:

  • the name you type and the picture you choose — one of the built-in faces, or a photo you upload (cut to a small square on your device before it is sent). An uploaded photo is sent to Google Cloud Vision to be checked for adult or violent content; only a photo that passes is stored for other players to see, and one that does not is refused;
  • your rating, games won and lost, tournament results, and game statistics;
  • your saved house rules;
  • the players you have muted or blacklisted, with their names as you last saw them.

Your name, picture, rating and tournament medals are public to other players: they are shown at tables, in the rated lounge, in friend lists and on the tournament leaderboard.

Playing online

Online tables, the rated lounge and tournaments use Unity's multiplayer services (Lobby and Relay). Your game traffic passes through Unity's servers, which see your network (IP) address as any internet service does. What the other players receive is what the game needs: your name, picture, rating, player ID, the cards you play and your bids. The cards in your hand are never sent to other players.

In the rated lounge, the people in it can see your name, picture, rating, which game you are looking for, your recent lounge chat and the player IDs on your blacklist (so the matchmaker can keep you apart). Lounge whispers are not private: every lounge member's device receives them, they are only not shown there.

Tournament leaderboards keep your player ID, points, name and picture for the month, and past months are archived.

Ratings are kept by the game's server, not by your device. For each rated game it records, privately, who sat where (player IDs), how each player's game reported the end of the game (the scores, and who won or who left) and the rating changes it made from those reports. It uses them only to settle ratings.

If you put one of your own games on the rated list, its name and rules, your player name and your player ID are kept on the list while people play it and shown to other players; it leaves the list after an hour with no game, and deleting your account takes it off.

Chat, voice and video

  • Table chat goes through the table's host to the people at the table. It is not stored by us; it disappears when the game ends.
  • Messages to friends are delivered through Unity's Friends service while your friend is online. The conversation history is kept only on your device and theirs.
  • Voice chat uses Vivox (a Unity service) and is streamed live to the people at your table. It is not recorded. The microphone is used only while your microphone button is on.
  • Video uses Agora and streams your camera live to the people at your table. It is not recorded. The camera is used only while your camera button is on, and it is off at the start of every game.

A host may turn chat, voice or video off at their table, and you can mute any player.

Table chat, lounge chat, messages and player names are filtered: offensive words are replaced with stars. When a line contains a slur, the game sends us that line, your player ID and where it was said, so repeated slurs can be warned about — the line is kept for up to two days.

Friends

If you add friends, Unity's Friends service stores your friend list, friend requests and the players you have blocked, and shows your friends whether you are online and whether you are at a table they can join.

Reports, warnings and bans

If you report a player, the game sends us your player ID, theirs, the reason you chose, any note you wrote and the last few chat lines your screen saw from them. Reports are stored privately in Unity Cloud Save, where only the game's server and its administrator can read them.

Breaking the rules counts as an offence: a photo refused by the check, repeated slurs, reports from several different players, or a decision by the administrator. Each offence in a year moves one step: a warning with chat off for 24 hours, then bans from online play of 3 days, 7 days and 30 days, then a permanent ban. Single-player always stays playable.

We keep each offence (when, why, and what caused it) for up to three years; only the last year counts. Whether you are currently banned or have chat turned off is visible to the game on other players' devices, so their tables can turn you away — the reason is not shown to them.

Permissions on your device

  • Microphone — only for voice chat, and only asked for the first time you turn it on.
  • Camera — only for video, and only asked for the first time you turn it on.
  • Photos / files — only when you choose to upload a picture; only the picture you pick is read.

On your device

Your settings, a single-player game in progress, cached copies of your profile and statistics, and your chat history with friends are kept on your device. Uninstalling the game removes them.

Who processes your information

  • Unity Technologies — sign-in, Cloud Save, multiplayer, Friends, leaderboards, Cloud Code and Vivox voice. Unity's privacy policy: https://unity.com/legal/privacy-policy
  • Agora — live video. Agora's privacy policy: https://www.agora.io/en/privacy-policy/

We do not share your information with anyone else, and we do not sell it.

Deleting your account

In the game: Online, then Account, then Delete account. This deletes your sign-in, your profile and approved photo, rating, statistics, saved house rules, mute and blacklist lists and friend list, and clears them from your device. It cannot be undone.

A few things are not removed by it: your entries on past tournament leaderboards, reports filed by or about you and any warnings or bans (kept for moderation), the server's records of rated games you played (they are other players' results too), and anything other players already received, such as chat on their screens. To have leaderboard entries removed too, or to ask for deletion without the game, write to jkirby1325@gmail.com with your player name.

Children

The game includes chat, voice and video with other players and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect information from children under 13; if you believe a child has made an account, write to jkirby1325@gmail.com and we will delete it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be in the game and at the same address, with a new effective date.
